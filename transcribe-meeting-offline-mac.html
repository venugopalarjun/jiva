How to transcribe a meeting offline on a Mac

Yes, a Mac with Apple silicon can turn speech into text with no internet connection. You can use Apple's own Notes or Voice Memos, run an open speech model such as Whisper, or use a meeting app built on such models. They differ in whether they hear both sides of a call and whether they tell speakers apart.

Option 1: Notes and Voice Memos

On recent versions of macOS, Notes can record audio and write a transcript, and Voice Memos can show a transcript of a recording. Apple describes it in Record and transcribe audio in Notes on Mac.

These are good for a talk, an interview in a room, or your own notes. They record through the microphone, so on a call with headphones they hear only you. They do not label who is speaking.

Option 2: an open speech model

Whisper is a speech model that OpenAI released openly, and there are free tools that run it on a Mac. You record the meeting some other way, then give the audio file to the model. The text is accurate, and nothing leaves your computer.

The cost is the work around it: you need a recording that has both sides of the call, you wait for the file to be processed, and plain Whisper does not say who spoke.

Option 3: a meeting app that runs on your Mac

A meeting app does the surrounding work: it records both sides of the call, transcribes while the meeting runs, separates the speakers and writes a summary. The thing to check is the word "local". Some apps record on your Mac but send the audio away to be transcribed.

How to check that an app is really offline

Turn Wi-Fi off and record a short call or a video playing on your Mac. If you still get a transcript and a summary, the work is done on your Mac. If it waits for a connection, it is not.

Where Jiva fits

Jiva is option 3. After a one-time download of its speech models (about 6 GB), recording, live transcription, speaker separation, summaries and search all work with no internet. Audio and transcripts are not uploaded, and there is no account.

Common questions

Is offline transcription as accurate as a cloud service?

It is close, and it depends on the audio. On one public test meeting Jiva got 21% of words wrong and a cloud notetaker got 17%, while Jiva gave far more words to the right speaker. The full numbers and method are on the Benchmarks page.

Which Macs can do it?

Local speech models run best on Apple silicon (an M-series chip). Jiva needs Apple silicon and macOS 14.4 or later; it does not run on Intel Macs.

Does it work in languages other than English?

It depends on the model. Whisper covers many languages. Check the app you choose for the languages it supports.
