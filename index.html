Install with one commandRecommended
$ curl -fsSL https://jiva.works/install.sh | bash
Downloads Jiva, verifies its checksum, installs it to your Applications, and opens it — no drag, no Gatekeeper prompt. Safe to re-run anytime; it skips the download if you're already current.
Prefer Homebrew?
$ brew install --cask venugopalarjun/jiva/jiva
Or by hand: use the Download for Mac button, drag Jiva to Applications, then run xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine /Applications/Jiva.app once in Terminal before opening it.

What it does

Four things, done well.

Names, not "Speaker 1"

Recognizes the same voice across meetings, so names stick.

Cited, not invented

Every summary and answer links to the moment it was said.

Live transcription

Both sides of the call, on-device. Starts and stops with the meeting.

Hold to dictate

Hold a key, speak in any app, and it types for you.

Straight answers

The questions people ask before installing.

Is it really free?

Yes. There's no paid tier and no account to create.

Does anything go to the cloud?

No. Recording, transcripts and search work offline on your Mac. The only exception is if you connect an AI app like Claude and ask it about your meetings: the passages it reads go to that app.

Do other people on the call know?

No bot joins the call. Jiva records your Mac's audio like QuickTime does, so telling people is up to you.

What if I share my screen?

Your private notes overlay doesn't appear in screen shares.

Why does Jiva ask for Google Calendar?

Optional, read-only access so Jiva can remind you before a meeting, open the call with one click, and name the recording. Your calendar is read on your Mac and never sent to Jiva. See the privacy policy.
